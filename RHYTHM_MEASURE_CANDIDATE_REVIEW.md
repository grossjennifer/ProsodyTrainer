# Candidate review for the new rhythm-sensitivity measure

## Recommended design target

For a 30-item measure with 20 multiple-pulse items and 10 single-pulse
items, use the following composition:

| Section | Content/content contrasts | Function/function contrasts | Legacy literary anchors | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Multiple pulse | 15 | 3 | 2 | 20 |
| Single pulse | 8 | 2 | 0 | 10 |

This keeps function-word promotion available for measurement without allowing
it to define the task. It also keeps the legacy literary material as a small
anchor set rather than making poem recognition the construct.

Do not use content/function or function/content contrasts in the final pool.
They make the marked word class itself a cue. Every target and foil should be
content/content or function/function.

## Current 40-item diagnostic pool

### Retain as prose candidates

The strongest starting points are the content/content items:

- M21, M22, M23, M24
- M31, M32
- M39, M40

These are ordinary-prose lines and the engine finds a clear intended contrast
for each. They should still receive human review for naturalness and for the
number and location of pulses.

M19 (the waltz line) should be treated as a special rhythmic anchor, not as an
ordinary prose item. If it remains, mark the dance rhythm at the syllable level
and verify that the foil preserves the same number of pulses. M20 is a useful
dactylic literary anchor, but it should not be counted as ordinary prose.

### Remove or rewrite

- M07–M18 and M27–M30 and M35–M38: these mix content targets with
  function-word foils or function targets with content foils. They should be
  rewritten as within-class contrasts or replaced.
- M01 and M02: recognizable literary or nonsense material, not good final
  prose items.
- M03–M06, M25–M26, M33–M34: function/function candidates that invite
  competing contextual readings such as *we will*, *you can*, and *they
  must*. They are useful exploratory probes, but they should not enter the
  final score unless pilot data show a clear shared interpretation.

## Legacy items to consider

Use no more than two legacy-derived passages in the scored pool. The best
starting candidates are:

1. QID54, *Lives of great men all remind us* — trochaic template.
2. QID67, *When Horton the elephant heard a small noise* — anapestic template,
   but retain its nearby dactylic ambiguity during review.

QID57 (*In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes*) is a reasonable reserve
iambic anchor. QID62 and QID66 remain promising, but their syllable-level
markings and matched foils need confirmation before they are scored.

The old strongest incongruent items (QID60, QID64, QID56, QID52, and QID51)
should not be copied directly as foils. They were different passages from the
congruent set, not matched versions of the same text.

## Item-writing rules for the next draft

1. Keep the original sentence unchanged; alter only the typographic marking.
2. Mark syllables, not whole words.
3. Give the key and foil the same number of marked pulses.
4. Keep the foil within the same word class as the key whenever possible.
5. Avoid adjacent marked syllables unless the item is an intentional,
   separately labeled probe.
6. Put at least one unmarked phrase before the first critical pulse so that
   participants can construct the rhythm before the contrast appears.
7. Use mostly natural prose, with two or three literary anchors at most.
