Implicit prosody

Implicit prosody is not a sixth item on that list. It is the claim that readers construct all five while reading silently, with none of it printed on the page — which is what the tools on this site try to make visible.

The page will cover what it means to say readers project prosody onto text, and how that idea is studied.

Where this came from

This site grew directly out of the research. Gross, Winegard, and Plotkowski (2018) concluded their study of stress marking in print by asking whether a free application could transform ordinary text into typographically enhanced text. They posed the question using the same stress-marking convention they had studied:

Can YOU iMAGine an APP that HELPS you HEAR the RHYTHm of TEXT?

As printed in the 2018 article. In ordinary capitalization: can you imagine an app that helps you hear the rhythm of text? The capitals mark stressed syllables — the same convention used in the article’s title, Marking stress exPLICitly, and the one the tools on this site use.

Prosody Trainer is one answer to that question.

Gross, J., Winegard, B., & Plotkowski, A. R. (2018). Marking stress exPLICitly in written English fosters rhythm in the reader’s inner voice. Reading Research Quarterly, 53(3), 305–321. https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.198 · Open access

The Stress page explains what that study found — and what it did not.
