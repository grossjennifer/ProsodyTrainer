Implicit prosody

Implicit prosody is not a sixth component. It is the claim that readers construct all five while reading silently, with none of it printed on the page — that silent reading has a sound structure of its own, which is what the tools on this site try to make visible.

The page covers what it means to say readers project prosody onto text, how researchers study something no one can hear, and what the evidence does and does not establish.

Where this came from

This site grew directly out of the research. Gross, Winegard, and Plotkowski (2018) concluded their study of stress marking in print by asking whether a free application could transform ordinary text into typographically enhanced text. They posed the question using the same stress-marking convention they had studied:

Can YOU iMAGine an APP that HELPS you HEAR the RHYTHm of TEXT?

As printed in the 2018 article, with capitals marking the stressed syllables: Can YOU iMAGine an APP that HELPS you HEAR the RHYTHm of TEXT?

As printed in the 2018 article. Unmarked, it reads: Can you imagine an app that helps you hear the rhythm of text? The capitals fall on stressed syllables, as in the article’s title — Marking stress exPLICitly — and as the tools on this site mark them.

Prosody Trainer is one answer to that question.

Gross, J., Winegard, B., & Plotkowski, A. R. (2018). Marking stress exPLICitly in written English fosters rhythm in the reader’s inner voice. Reading Research Quarterly, 53(3), 305–321. https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.198 · Open access
