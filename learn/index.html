Prosody

The sound structure of language: several distinct things, often discussed as one.

  • Stress — which syllable of a word carries the beat
  • Rhythm — how those beats distribute across a phrase
  • Phrasing — which words group together
  • Emphasis and focus — which word carries the news
  • Intonation — how pitch rises and falls across the groupin development

Implicit prosody is not a sixth item on that list. It is the claim that readers construct all of the above while reading silently, with none of it printed on the page — which is what the tools on this site try to make visible.in development

Where this came from

This site grew directly out of the research. Gross, Winegard, and Plotkowski (2018) closed their study of stress marking in print by asking whether a free application could be built that would turn ordinary text into typographically enhanced text. They posed the question in marked capitals themselves: could you imagine an app that helps you hear the rhythm of text? Prosody Trainer is one answer to that question.

Gross, J., Winegard, B., & Plotkowski, A. R. (2018). Marking stress exPLICitly in written English fosters rhythm in the reader’s inner voice. Reading Research Quarterly, 53(3), 305–321. https://doi.org/10.1002/rrq.198 Open access. The Stress page explains what that study found — and what it did not.
